# Quantitative Finance

## Overview
Quantitative finance is an interdisciplinary field that combines mathematics, statistics, economics, and computer science to analyze and model financial markets and investments. Its primary goal is to understand, quantify, and manage financial risk. This reference page compiles key concepts, techniques, and insights from the course "The Mathematics of Market Dynamics: State Machines and Quantitative Trading" to provide a comprehensive understanding of the topic.

## Key Concepts

* **State-space theory**: A mathematical framework used to model complex systems by defining their possible states and the rules governing transitions between them. In finance, state-space theory can be applied to understand market dynamics and predict regime changes.

* **Markov property**: A stochastic process where the future state depends only on the current state and not on the sequence of events that preceded it. This concept is fundamental to understanding financial time series and predicting market behavior.

* **Regime changes**: Transitions in market dynamics, characterized by shifts in volatility, correlations, or other key market indicators. Modeling regime changes is crucial for effective risk management and trading strategies.

* **Financial time series**: A sequence of data points, measured typically at successive times, spaced at uniform time intervals. Examples include daily stock prices or monthly interest rates.

## Techniques & Methods

* **State machines**: A mathematical model used to represent a sequence of states and transitions between them. In the context of finance, state machines can be used to model market regimes and predict regime changes.

* **Hidden Markov models (HMMs)**: A statistical model that assumes the underlying system being modeled is a Markov process with unobserved (hidden) states. HMMs can be applied to financial time series to predict regime changes and optimize trading strategies.

* **Maximum likelihood estimation (MLE)**: A method for estimating the parameters of a statistical model, given some observed data. In finance, MLE can be used to estimate the parameters of a HMM, providing insights into market dynamics and regime changes.

## Insights & Lessons Learned

1. Financial markets are not purely random events, but rather complex systems with discernible, predictable patterns.
2. State-space theory provides a powerful framework for understanding and modeling market dynamics.
3. Hidden Markov models can be used to predict regime changes, enabling more effective risk management and trading strategies.
4. Maximum likelihood estimation is a valuable tool for estimating the parameters of HMMs and understanding market behavior.
5. Understanding the mathematical underpinnings of financial markets is essential for advanced quantitative finance.
