## Core Concepts

### The Importance of Transparency in Trading
Transparency in financial reporting is the foundation of trust and accurate performance assessment. For a trader, transparent tracking of Profit and Loss (P&L) allows for objective self-evaluation, rather than relying on subjective feelings. This principle dictates that all realized gains and losses must be meticulously documented to understand the true efficacy of a trading strategy. Without this level of openness, traders cannot accurately identify patterns, correct flaws, or build a reliable knowledge base from their experiences.

### Quantifying Trailing Performance
Trailing performance is a crucial metric used to measure a trader's consistency and resilience over a defined period, rather than focusing only on a single trade's outcome. By calculating trailing performance, traders can assess whether their strategy is robust across varying market conditions. This metric shifts the focus from short-term fluctuations to long-term cumulative gains, providing a clearer picture of overall success and risk management effectiveness.

### The Impact of External Volatility
Market performance is invariably influenced by external macroeconomic factors and geopolitical events. Events like the volatility arising from the US-Iran war introduce unpredictable risk into trading environments. Understanding how external volatility affects realized profits is vital, as it helps traders differentiate between performance caused by skill and performance caused by external, uncontrollable forces. A skilled trader learns to manage risk irrespective of global events.

## Common Pitfalls / What to Watch Out For
The primary pitfalls for traders are allowing emotion to dictate decisions and failing to maintain transparency in their tracking. A common mistake is confusing short-term volatility with long-term strategy; external events can cause temporary losses, but a disciplined trader must not let fear derail the overall plan. Another pitfall is ignoring the psychological component—believing that strategy alone guarantees success, neglecting the fact that conviction determines execution quality. Traders must be careful not to rely solely on anecdotal wins, but use the structured data to verify the actual impact of their strategies.
