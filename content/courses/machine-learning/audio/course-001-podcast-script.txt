[SAM]: And the 'actions'? What are the actual choices an agent can make in that state?

[ALEX]: Actions, $A$, are the set of decisions available to the agent. In trading, these are the concrete moves: buy a specific quantity, sell a quantity, or hold the current position. The choice of action is what the MDP is designed to optimize.

[ALEX]: The reward is the outcome. It’s the feedback mechanism that tells the agent whether the action taken led to a positive or negative result. The goal of the entire MDP is to find the optimal sequence of actions that maximizes the total accumulated reward over the trading period, balancing risk and return.

[SAM]: That makes sense—the reward is the objective function. But the core magic of the MDP, the part that separates it from simple reactive algorithms, is the Markov property, right?

[ALEX]: Exactly. The Markov property is the defining feature. It means that the probability of transitioning to any future state depends *only* on the current state and the action taken, and is entirely independent of the past history of states and actions. It simplifies the immense complexity of the market by ensuring that future predictions only rely on the immediate context.

[ALEX]: Precisely. By enforcing the Markov property, we manage the complexity. It allows us to define the environment as dynamic and stochastic, making it the perfect framework for modeling financial markets. It ties the decision-making process directly into probability theory and dynamic programming.
