## Background & Context
Non-Resident Bearer Bonds (NRBBs) were first issued in the 1930s in the United States as a means for non-residents to invest in the country's debt securities. However, the use of bearer bonds has been largely discontinued due to their association with money laundering and tax evasion. ## Core Concepts

### NRBBEARING
NRBBEARING, or Non-Resident Bearer Bonds, are financial instruments that are not registered in the name of the holder. This means that the owner of the bond is the person who physically possesses the bond, rather than being recorded in a registry. This anonymity made NRBBEARINGs attractive to non-residents looking to invest in a country without disclosing their identity.

### Non-Resident
Non-residents are individuals who do not reside in the country where the bonds are issued. In the context of NRBBEARINGs, non-residents were typically foreign investors looking to invest in the United States' debt securities.

### Bearer Bonds
Bearer bonds are financial instruments where the ownership is determined by the physical possession of the document. These bonds do not have a registered owner and are easily transferable, making them attractive for those seeking anonymity.
