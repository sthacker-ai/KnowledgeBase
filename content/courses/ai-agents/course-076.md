# How to Build a Managed AI Agent Business Solo

## Overview
This comprehensive course teaches you how to build and manage an AI agent business for customers, providing a digital employee that knows their business and continuously improves. We cover the offer, the tools and stacks, onboarding customers, and selling to executives, agencies, and law firms.

## Background & Context
- AI agents are in high demand, with businesses willing to pay up to $5,000 a month per customer for managed services.
- The opportunity lies in creating a frictionless experience for customers, who don't want to deal with tokens, infrastructure, or maintenance.
- This guide is based on a video by Greg Isenberg, featuring Nick from Orgo, sharing insights from their own successful AI agent business.

## Core Concepts

### Managed AI Agent Business
A business model in which a solo entrepreneur builds, manages, and improves AI agents for customers, providing a digital employee that understands the customer's business and continuously improves.

### Frictionless Offer
An offer that removes all complexities and concerns for customers, including tokens, infrastructure, security, and ongoing maintenance.

### Vertical Specialization
Focusing on specific industries and sub-niches to create clarity and value for customers.

## How It Works / Step-by-Step

### Creating the Offer
1. Offer unlimited agents, unlimited usage, monitoring, support, security, and ongoing changes.
2. Customers don't actually need unlimited agents; one or two well-configured agents can provide significant value.
3. Charge $5,000 a month for the service.

### Onboarding Customers
1. Clarify that you're selling an AI employee, not just an AI agent.
2. Claim that you can solve problems in the customer's business using AI.
3. Focus on business outcomes rather than time saved.
4. Ensure a seamless experience and rapid deployment (within 48 hours).

### Selecting Industries and Niches
1. Focus on industries with legacy systems and a desire to adopt AI.
2. Examples of suitable industries: marketing agencies, law firms, insurance agencies, manufacturers, wholesalers, and real estate agencies.
3. After selecting an industry, narrow down to a sub-niche or sub-category, e.g., commercial real estate agencies in Florida.

### Common Patterns
- Decision-makers in target industries often struggle with similar problems.
- Building rapport with executives and decision-makers is crucial.

## Real-World Examples & Use Cases
- A marketing agency wants to automate their content creation process and improve efficiency.
- A law firm seeks to streamline document review and analysis.
- An insurance agency aims to reduce manual work and improve customer service.

## Key Insights & Takeaways
- Focus on providing a frictionless experience for customers.
- Sell AI employees, not AI agents.
- Business outcomes are more important than time saved.
- Rapid deployment and clear communication are essential.
- Specialize in industries and sub-niches with a high need for AI solutions.

## Common Pitfalls / What to Watch Out For
- Avoid industries with high regulatory burdens and red tape.
- Don't oversell the number of agents needed; focus on the value provided by one or two well-configured agents.
- Be cautious when niching down; ensure there's a sufficient market and demand for your services.
