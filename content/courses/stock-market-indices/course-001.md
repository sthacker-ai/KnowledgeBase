## Background & Context
The stock market indices, such as the MSCI, serve as benchmark indicators for evaluating the performance and risk of global equity markets. When large institutional bodies, such as asset managers and pension funds, use these indices to allocate capital, changes to the index composition—a process known as a "re-jig"—trigger significant shifts in investment flows. This phenomenon is critical because these flows determine the supply and demand dynamics in specific markets, influencing asset prices and overall economic sentiment. The context here is centered on the dynamic relationship between a specific country's status (India) and the global indexing system (MSCI), which subsequently affects international investment decisions, particularly concerning passive, long-term investment strategies.

## Core Concepts

### MSCI Re-jig
The MSCI (Morgan Stanley Capital International) indices are a set of global equity indices used worldwide to track the performance of various global markets. A "re-jig" refers to a structural adjustment or re-calibration of the index composition, which involves adding, removing, or reclassifying countries or sectors based on specific criteria (like ESG standards, market capitalization, or liquidity). When the MSCI performs a re-jig, it signals a change in the perceived attractiveness and risk profile of the underlying markets, which instantly affects the quantum of capital available for investment.

### Foreign Passive Outflows from India
Passive outflows refer to the movement of investment capital where investors are not actively trading or taking speculative positions but are simply adjusting their portfolios based on the performance and perceived risk of an asset. Foreign passive outflows from India specifically describe the redirection of funds away from the Indian market by international investors, driven by the structural changes or risk assessment signaled by the MSCI re-jig. This movement is typically driven by macro-economic factors, currency movements, and the altered risk-reward profile established by the index adjustments.
