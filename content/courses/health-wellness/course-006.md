- When adding new images, there is a tendency to let earlier images fade; the key is to periodically scan the mental field and reinstate any lost items before proceeding.  
- Overloading too quickly—adding many new images at once—can cause cognitive overload and discouragement; incremental increases of three images at a time are recommended.  
- Mistaking the exercise for a purely visual task neglects the role of imagination and creativity; practitioners should actively engage in combining or interacting the images to deepen the experience.  
- Expecting immediate, dramatic “third‑eye opening” experiences can lead to disappointment; the benefits accrue gradually through consistent practice.  
- Practicing in a noisy or visually stimulating environment undermines the ability to generate clear internal imagery; a quiet, dimly lit space supports better results.  
- Failing to reflect on the difficulty level after each round prevents accurate self‑assessment; asking the probing questions from the transcript is crucial for tracking progress.
