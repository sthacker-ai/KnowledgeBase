# Machine Learning

Machine learning is a subset of artificial intelligence (AI) that provides systems the ability to automatically learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed. It focuses on the development of computer programs that can access data and use it to learn for themselves. This comprehensive reference page explores key concepts, techniques, and insights related to machine learning, drawing from five expert-led courses.

## Key Concepts

### Markov Decision Processes (MDPs)
MDPs are a mathematical framework for modeling decision-making in situations where outcomes are partly random and partly under the control of a decision-maker. They are used to optimize sequential decisions under uncertainty, making them particularly useful in systematic trading strategies. [[Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) for Systematic Trading Decisions]]

### Large Language Models (LLMs)
LLMs are AI models that can understand and generate human-like text based on the input they receive. They are trained on vast amounts of data and can perform a wide range of natural language processing tasks, such as translation, summarization, and question-answering. [[The Economics and Competitive Landscape of Large Language Models]], [[Building a Permanent AI‑Powered Knowledge Base with Karpathy’s LLM Wiki Approach]], [[The Atom of LLMs: Understanding Tokens and Tokenization]]

### Tokens and Tokenization
Tokens are the fundamental units of text that LLMs process. Tokenization is the process of converting raw text into numerical tokens, which enables LLMs to understand and process human language. Mastering tokenization is essential for debugging and understanding LLMs. [[The Atom of LLMs: Understanding Tokens and Tokenization]]

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
RAG is a machine learning technique that combines retrieval (searching a corpus for relevant information) and generation (producing new text based on that information) to create a more effective AI model. It can be used to build a self-improving, AI-driven knowledge base. [[Building a Permanent AI‑Powered Knowledge Base with Karpathy’s LLM Wiki Approach]]

### Self-Hosting LLMs
Self-hosting LLMs involves running powerful, proprietary models, like OpenAI Codex, on a user's local machine. This approach can eliminate API costs, bypass rate limits, and ensure complete data privacy. [[Self-Hosting Large Language Models: Running OpenAI Codex Locally with Ollama]]

## Insights & Lessons Learned

1. Mastering tokenization is crucial for understanding and debugging LLMs, as it forms the foundation of their behavior, efficiency, and debugging.
2. MDPs provide a rigorous foundation for making optimal sequential decisions in complex, stochastic environments, which is essential for systematic trading strategies.
3. The cost of training state-of-the-art LLMs can reach hundreds of millions of dollars, highlighting the significant capital required to achieve top performance.
4. Running LLMs locally can address concerns regarding data privacy, latency, and cost, making it an increasingly popular approach in the AI landscape.
5. The development of LLMs is not just a scientific endeavor; it is a massive economic undertaking, driven by the necessity to train models with exponentially increasing parameters and vast amounts of data.
